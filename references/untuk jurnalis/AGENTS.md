# SISJ Metascience/Open Science Workshop — Progress Notes

## Context
- **Organizer:** Indonesian Society of Science Journalists (SISJ)
- **Format:** ~2 hour mini workshop
- **Audience:** 5 fellowship program participants, mostly master's degree holders, various disciplines (science journalists, not necessarily metascience specialists)
- **Topic:** Introduction to metascience / open science
- **Core framing (from Amelia, 2026-09-13):**
  1. Metascience/open science as a **practical toolkit to assess the validity of scientific claims** (e.g. when covering a study or press release)
  2. **Basic "forensic metascience" skills** so participants can conduct their own investigative work (spotting red flags, checking reproducibility/integrity signals)

  1. **Schapals et al. (2026), "Shining Light on 'Dark Science'"** (Media and Communication) — maps a 5-type global taxonomy of "science watchdog journalism" actors (primary investigative, infrastructural platforms, translational intermediaries, corrective verification, attention-based). Notably includes Global South watchdog examples (Plagio SOS/Latin America, India Research Watch, Romania's Pandora Project), validating our push to add Indonesian cases.   2. **Bottesini, Aschwanden, Rhemtulla & Vazire (2023), "How Do Science Journalists Evaluate Psychology Research?"** (AMPPS) — preregistered experiment, 181 science journalists rating fictitious psychology vignettes. Sample size was the only factor with a robust effect on trustworthiness/newsworthiness ratings; university prestige had none. Qualitative data flagged journal-prestige-as-quality-proxy (empirically weak per cited Brembs et al. 2013), experimental-vs-correlational design, and independent expert comment-seeking as things journalists say they rely on.
